# User guide

WinDictate Restricted Pilot {{VERSION}}.

## Before you start

This pilot tool is for **dictating after the event**: notes after a meeting, a draft e-mail, an update to a record. Do not use it to record live conversations, calls or meetings. **Read every dictated sentence before you save, send or submit it**: speech recognition can mishear names, numbers and specialist terms.

## Dictate

1. Click where the text should go (an e-mail, a document, a form field).
2. **Hold Ctrl+Alt** (bottom-left of the keyboard). A small pill appears just above the taskbar, with moving bars and the name of the app your text will go into, for example "Outlook".
3. **Speak** naturally, in full sentences.
4. **Let go.** The pill shows three dots while it works, then a tick, and the text appears where your cursor is, followed by a space.

Each dictation can be up to 5 minutes long. You can start the next dictation while the previous one is still being worked on; they go in in the order you spoke them.

**To cancel**, press **Esc** before you let go. Nothing is inserted, and the audio is thrown away.

## Check what you dictated

- Names, amounts, account numbers, dates and acronyms are the most likely to be wrong. Read them carefully.
- Punctuation is added automatically and may need adjusting.
- **Custom words** (Settings > Advanced) can help with product names and acronyms you use often. Do not put client names there.

## What the messages mean

| Message | What to do |
|---|---|
| "Didn't catch that" | Nothing was heard. Hold a moment longer and speak up |
| "Copied. Press Ctrl+V" | The text could not be put in (for example Windows refused the keystrokes). It is on the clipboard: click where it should go and press Ctrl+V within 2 minutes |
| "Focus moved. Press Ctrl+V" | You clicked into another window or field before the text was ready, so it was not typed there. Go back and press Ctrl+V |
| "Partly typed. Check, then Ctrl+V" | Windows stopped accepting keystrokes part-way. Delete the partial text and press Ctrl+V |
| "Check it arrived. If not, Ctrl+V" | The paste may not have gone in: no text field had the cursor, or Windows only partly accepted the keystrokes. Look first, so you do not paste it twice; if it is not there, click where it should go and press Ctrl+V |
| "Microphone disconnected" | The headset was unplugged or stopped working. Reconnect it and dictate again |
| "Microphone wasn't ready" / "Microphone not responding" | The microphone took too long to start. Wait for the moving bars before speaking, or choose another microphone in Settings |
| "Cancelled" | You pressed another key, or Windows showed a security screen, while you were holding the shortcut |

## The text went nowhere?

If you let go while the cursor was not in a text box, WinDictate notices that nothing took the text, says "Check it arrived. If not, Ctrl+V", and leaves it on the clipboard for 2 minutes. If a dictation went somewhere you did not mean, right-click the WinDictate icon in the notification area (next to the clock) within 2 minutes, choose **Copy last dictation**, then click where it should go and press Ctrl+V.

## The tray icon

Right-click the microphone icon next to the clock:

| Item | What it does |
|---|---|
| Enabled | Untick to pause dictation (for example during a call); tick to resume |
| Copy last dictation | Puts your last dictation on the clipboard (available for 2 minutes) |
| Settings… | Indicator style, microphone, typing or pasting, custom words |
| About… | Version, build and privacy details; the pilot notice |
| Open log | The event log, for support (it never contains what you said) |
| Exit | Stops WinDictate completely |

## Settings

- **Indicator**: *Glass pill* (default: shows the target app's name) or *Edge glow* (the top edge of the taskbar lights up; no words). **Preview** shows it.
- **Space after each dictation**: on by default so sentences do not run together.
- **Microphone**: **Test** shows a moving bar when it hears you; nothing is recorded. Choose a specific microphone under Advanced.
- **Advanced**: shortcut keys (if Ctrl+Alt clashes with another program), ignoring quick taps, pasting or typing, microphone, custom words.
- **Privacy (fixed in this pilot)**: no history, the clipboard is always put back, no network. These cannot be changed.

## Good habits

- Dictate in a quiet, private place, with a headset if possible.
- Pause WinDictate (untick **Enabled**) during calls and meetings.
- Keep sentences short and speak numbers clearly ("one five zero zero pounds").
- Read before you send.

## Help

If WinDictate says the speech model is missing or failed its check, or that the PC is not supported, contact the pilot support contact listed by your pilot team. About > **Copy details** copies the version information they need.
